Method

How this register decides what counts, and how it is checked.

Success here is not coverage — commercial databases list more companies and more money. Success is that any figure on this site can be checked against its source in one click. This page states the rules that make that possible.

What counts as a unicorn here

A company is included only if all three of the following hold:

  1. Founded in Germany, or headquartered in Germany. This is recorded explicitly per company, so dual-HQ cases — Celonis is headquartered in both Munich and New York — are visible rather than hidden. Being German only by investor base does not qualify.
  2. Currently independent and private. Not IPO'd, not acquired, not insolvent.
  3. Reached a publicly reported post-money valuation of at least $1B or at least €1B, in the currency the source used. Either threshold qualifies — a company reported at "$1B" is included without being silently restated in euros — and a company's own figures are never converted between currencies.

Why every number has a date

"Current unicorn" is not an observable fact. Private valuations are stale by construction — a company last valued during a 2022 round may be worth something different today, and there is no public market price to check against in between. This site does not try to resolve that; it exposes it. Every valuation renders with the date it was reported, and a company's unicorn status is determined by its last public valuation, not by a current guess.

aged A valuation more than 24 months old is marked this way in the grid. The 24-month window is measured from the newest source publication date in the whole dataset, not from today — data/companies.json is generated and committed, and has to build to the exact same bytes no matter what day the build runs, so nothing in it may depend on the wall clock.

Disputed works differently: when a new figure conflicts with data already on file, it is recorded alongside the existing figure and flagged as disputed rather than silently overwriting it. The record keeps both claims and their sources so a reader can judge which to trust, instead of the site quietly picking one on their behalf.

Where the numbers come from

Every figure carries a source, and every source must be on a short, deliberately restrictive allowlist:

  • Primary: company and investor press releases, and the official registries Handelsregister and Bundesanzeiger.
  • Trade press: Gründerszene, Sifted, EU-Startups, Tech.eu, TechCrunch.
  • Business press with editorial standards: Handelsblatt, Reuters, Bloomberg, Financial Times.

A source must also carry a genuine publication date. Continuously-updated pages with no publication date — live company profiles, "about us" pages, real-time rankings — are not citable, because a page's access date is not the same thing as the date a figure was reported.

What is excluded, and why

Licensed-database profile pages — Crunchbase, Dealroom, PitchBook, Tracxn — are excluded without exception. This is a terms-of-service and redistribution problem, not a judgement on their quality: their data is licensed for viewing, not for republication, and this register republishes every figure it lists. Tracxn in particular is a useful model for what a profile page should show; it is still not a citable source here. Wikipedia and aggregator or SEO-republisher sites carrying no original reporting are excluded for the more direct reason that they are not where a figure was first reported.

Precision

Figures like ~€13 bn and dates like Mar 2024 are deliberately imprecise, matching the precision the source actually gave. Rounding a "nearly $13 billion" valuation to a false decimal, or padding a month-only date to a day the source never stated, would manufacture certainty that does not exist. The site does not claim precision it does not have.

The one conversion that reaches a reader, and the one that doesn't

No figure shown to a reader is ever converted between currencies — a valuation reported in dollars stays in dollars everywhere it is displayed: on that company's profile, in the grid, and in the timeline of its rounds. What you read is what the source said, in the currency the source used.

A fixed USD → EUR rate exists in the dataset for two internal uses, neither of which changes a displayed figure. The first is the register's headline "combined value" statistic, which adds every company's valuation into a single number and has to pick one currency to add them in. The second is the sort key behind ordering the grid by valuation: ranking a mixed-currency list requires a common unit, so a converted figure is computed to sort by — and only to sort by. It is never rendered. The rate is disclosed here: 1 USD = — EUR, as of —. The combined figure is labelled approximate for exactly this reason.

Corrections

Every change to the dataset is a reviewable diff against a per-company JSON file — there is no hidden edit path. If a figure is wrong, out of date, or missing its source, open an issue on the GitHub issue tracker, or use the "Report an error" link in the footer of any page.
