Everything important, kept short.

What this register is, where every figure comes from, and what to do when one of them is wrong.

Why does this website exist?

Germany's billion-euro companies sit behind paywalled databases and scattered press releases, and no public list carries the evidence with them. This is that list, 40 companies past a billion and 12 of them in the last twelve months, alongside the funding rounds German founders close week by week.

Where does the data come from?

Every figure traces to a dated, public page from an allowlisted set of publications, and a validator refuses to publish a number unless the quote behind it states that number. A company announcing its own figure outranks a report of it. Company figures stay in the currency their source used, so nothing is converted twice.

The one exception is the combined value on the front page, which converts USD at 0.92 (2026-08) and covers only the companies whose valuation a source has published.

How are the weekly funding rounds sourced?

Every round carries a dated link to the reporting behind it, from the same allowlisted publications as the register. The standard is lighter than the register's, in that the amount is sourced and linked rather than quote-checked, and where a weekly recap named no founders or city those come from elsewhere while the link stays on the round. The week is a selection of German rounds and not a complete list of them.

How often does this update?

Every Monday evening a scan reads the allowlisted feeds for the week that just ended, and reports whatever it found. The round-up is then written up, checked against the sources and published, usually within a few days. Every eighth week the same scan sweeps the whole register for what no funding wire reports, a company that listed, was acquired, or has simply gone quiet.

A week nobody raised in is a week with no entry, and a busy week can still wait to be written, so treat this as roughly weekly rather than punctually weekly. What you will not find here is an entry nobody wrote, which is the trade being made.

What does Undisclosed mean?

Some companies everyone agrees are unicorns have never had a figure published about them. Those entries print Undisclosed with a marker showing they are over the threshold, plus the sentence that establishes it and a link to the page it came from. They are left out of the combined value rather than counted as nothing.

What does Disputed mean?

Sources sometimes measure the same round differently, and a widely repeated number can be counting debt, acquisition currency or enterprise value. Where that gap would change what a reader believes, the register publishes the figure it can stand behind and records the other one beside it with its own link. It never picks one silently.

What does Reported mean?

Other trackers count more German unicorns than this register publishes, because most of them estimate valuations and this one only prints figures somebody published. A company carries Reported when a credible outside count treats it as a unicorn and that standard could not be met here, and the entry says in each case what is established and what is not. Reported companies are shown, marked, left out of the combined value, and counted separately in the summary, so the number at the top of the page never claims more than the evidence does.

What does the aged marker mean?

A valuation earns it when there is positive reason to think it has been overtaken, either because the company raised again more than two years after the figure was struck and nobody restated the price, or because the figure is more than five years old. It marks a number that is correctly sourced and probably behind the times, and it is deliberately rare, because a badge on half the register would be wallpaper.

How is this built?

Research and drafting are done with AI, the operator of this page reviews every change before it is published, and a validator blocks anything whose source does not state the figure. The only thing that runs on a schedule is the scan that reports there is work to do, and it publishes nothing.

The data is one JSON file per company in a public Git repository rather than a database, with the tooling around it written in Python. The site itself is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework and no build step, served as static files, so it costs nothing to run and every change is a readable diff. The two typefaces are self-hosted rather than loaded from a font CDN.

Who runs this and how do I report an error?

Lan Lukas Welge, in Berlin. If something here is wrong, out of date or missing a source, open an issue on the GitHub issue tracker. Corrections are the point, not an inconvenience.
