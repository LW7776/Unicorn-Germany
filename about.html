About

Everything important, kept short.

Why do unicorns matter?

They are the visible end of a capital pipeline. A country that cannot produce them loses its best companies to whoever will fund them.

Is the momentum real?

11 of these 32 companies crossed a billion in the last twelve months. That is not a backlog clearing. It is a pipeline running.

What would help most?

Growth capital at scale, and one European legal home instead of twenty-seven. Mario Draghi's report on European competitiveness put a number on the first, and Germany's WIN-Initiative, the Zukunftsfinanzierungsgesetz and the Commission's Savings and Investments Union are the instruments now aimed at it. The second is what more than 26,000 founders and investors asked for under the banner EU Inc and what the Commission's proposed 28th company regime, a draft regulation rather than law, would deliver.

Where does the data come from?

Every figure traces to a dated, public page from an allowlisted set of publications, and a validator refuses to publish a number unless the quote behind it states that number. Company figures stay in the currency their source used, so nothing is converted twice. The one exception is the combined value on the front page, which converts USD at 0.92 (2026-08) and covers only the companies whose valuation a source has published.

How often does this update?

Nothing here updates itself. Every Monday evening a scan reads the allowlisted feeds for the week that just ended, and if it found anything it says so. The round-up is then written by hand, checked against the sources and published, usually within a few days. Every eighth week the same scan sweeps the whole register for what no funding wire reports, a company that listed, was acquired, or has simply gone quiet. A week nobody raised in is a week with no entry, and a busy week can still wait for the person writing it, so treat this as roughly weekly rather than punctually weekly. What you will not find is a week nobody wrote, which is the trade being made: no machine-generated list of rounds has ever been published here, and none will be.

What does Undisclosed mean?

Some companies everyone agrees are unicorns have never had a figure published about them. Those entries print Undisclosed with a marker showing they are over the threshold, plus the sentence that establishes it and a link to the page it came from. They are left out of the combined value rather than counted as nothing.

Why do some figures carry a disputed note?

Sources sometimes measure the same round differently, and a widely repeated number can be counting debt, acquisition currency or enterprise value. Where that gap would change what a reader believes, the register publishes the figure it can stand behind and records the other one beside it with its own link. It never picks one silently.

How is this built?

Research and drafting are done with AI, a person reviews every change before it is published, and a validator blocks anything whose source does not state the figure. The only thing that runs on a schedule is the scan that says there is work to do, and it publishes nothing. The data is one JSON file per company in a public Git repository rather than a database, with the tooling around it written in Python. The site itself is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework and no build step, served as static files, so it costs nothing to run and every change is a readable diff.

Who runs this and how do I report an error?

Lan Lukas Welge, in Berlin. If something here is wrong, out of date or missing a source, open an issue on the GitHub issue tracker or use the "Report an error" link in the footer of any page. Corrections are the point, not an inconvenience.
